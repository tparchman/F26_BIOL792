## Course Description 
Modern biology is increasingly shaped by data sets far larger than most biologists are trained to work with, a challenge shared across many scientific disciplines. This is especially true in genomics, where advances in DNA sequencing technology have reduced sequencing time and cost by more than 10,000-fold. Traditional spreadsheet software and graphical statistical packages (e.g., Statistica, JMP) are inadequate for handling data of this scale.

This course introduces students to essential computational tools (Linux and Python) to increase proficiency in data manipulation and analysis, with a focus on biological data but broad applicability to any large data set. Students will gain hands-on experience analyzing their own research data as well as genomic data sets generated in my lab. The course provides a foundation for further skill development in programming and large-scale data analysis and is designed for graduate students in any scientific discipline that works with big data.

This year’s course will actively explore how artificial intelligence (AI) tools can be integrated into data science workflows. Students will learn how AI can accelerate the acquisition of programming and analytical skills, enhance the efficiency and reproducibility of research tasks, and assist in data visualization and interpretation. We will discuss both the capabilities and limitations of AI, emphasizing responsible and ethical use in scientific research. Importantly, students with foundational understanding of Linux and Python will be far better positioned to apply AI tools to accelerate research workflows with greater responsibility, flexibility, and efficiency.

During 2026, we will put effort into de-mystifying high-performance computing, and students will receive training in the use of such systems. We will spend several weeks introducing and using UNRs pronghorn HPC to understand system architecture, account maintenance, and job scheduling and deployment, with the goal of students feeling enabled for the future use of this and other such systems to enhance research productivity.


Prerequisites: must be enrolled as M.S. or Ph.D. student.


## Student Learning Outcomes
By the end of the semester, students should have learned enough to
start working more confidently with Linux and Python, and to have the basic toolset to learn independently moving forward. We will emphasize tools often used in genomics and bioinformatics, but the applications learned will apply generally to data science applications for any field. This course should also prepare students for the more advanced future courses.

* Use Linux environments to streamline data exploration, manipulation, and analysis.
     
* Write basic Python scripts and leverage libraries and packages to perform a wide range of operations and analyses.

* Access and utilize the High-Performance Computing resources at UNR to support graduate research objectives.

* Apply tools to their own research data, or data from related disciplines, to improve research

## Grades

Your grade in this course will be based on the following:
* **Weekly assignments (50%)** Assignments will involve working in the Linux environment, writing simple Bash and Python scripts, and working with a variety of  data sets that will be provided over the course of the semester. Assignments will be evaluated based on completion and effort. You can work in teams of 2 or 3 but will turn in your own python or bash scripts, or jupyter notebooks for each assignment. Code should be annotated, step by step, to explain what you did to complete the task. More guidelines on these files and each specific assignment will be available on the course website. Assignments will be due before class on Tuesdays unless otherwise specified.
 
* **Participation (30%)** This is a graduate course, with full attendance and participation expected. Participation entails **showing up for class prepared** and doing your best to work through assigned tasks and programming example problems. Some of the material we cover might be easy and quick to figure out. Other material and tasks will present roadblocks that will be more difficult to figure out. No questions will be stupid questions, and we will discuss and explore the use of AI to speed your learning.
 
* **Independent project (20%)** Everyone will be responsible for an independent project which can be organized either individually, or as a group. This will involve identifying a task or problem in your research (or the research of the group you work within) that either requires, or can be made much more efficient, using Python or Linux scripting. Each group (or individual) will need to turn in a one to two page description of the task and how they will solve it by **week 6**. By week 13, each group will need to turn in final scripts and a one to three page description of how the problem was solved, and how the code works. In addition, each group will give a short (10-15 minute) presentation that describes the data, the problem, and how their scripting tools work. For those without data or ideas, I can supply options.
